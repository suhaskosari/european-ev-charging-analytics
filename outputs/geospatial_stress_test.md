# Geospatial Analysis Stress Test

_Auto-generated by `python/eval_geospatial.py`. Asks whether the 'expansion candidates' reflect demand or just the geometry of the station layout._

Stations: **80** across **8** cities. Observed candidates at 3 km: **2** of 16 centroids.

## 1. Does demand weighting matter? (permutation null)

Session volumes were shuffled across stations within each city 200 times (destroying any location-demand link) and the analysis re-run.

- Candidates under the null: mean **2.69**, 5th-95th percentile **1-5**.
- Observed: **2**. One-sided permutation p-value: **0.82**.
- The observed count is typical of the null: the candidates are not explained by demand.

## 2. Is station demand spatial in this data?

Spearman correlation between a station's session volume and its distance from the city centre: pooled rho = **+0.17** (p = 0.14); per-city rho ranges **-0.38 to +0.72** with only 10 stations per city. In the generator, session volume depends on a station's power rating and connector count, never on its location, so there is no spatial demand to find.

## Conclusion

- Treat the expansion analysis as a **method demonstration** (clustering, haversine coverage, map output), not as evidence about where a real network should expand.
- Station coordinates are synthetic unless `OPENCHARGEMAP_API_KEY` is set, and even with real coordinates, session volumes here do not depend on location.
- A defensible real-world version would need demand observed or modelled *away from existing stations* (population, traffic, failed-session or waiting-time data), because demand measured at stations can only show where chargers already are.
